AI investors committed nearly $4 billion in a single October week. 💰
This pace is unlike anything I've tracked in years.

We're seeing money flow into three distinct bets right now.
Model building, vertical AI applications, and hardware infrastructure.
Each reflects a theory of where the real value lands.

TypeSafe AI closed an $870M Series A at a $7.5B valuation on October 9.
a16z led, with Sequoia and DCVC participating.
Jev launched just 24 days before this round closed.
Legora, a legal AI company, raised $550M at an $8.8B valuation.
Nvidia and Atlassian are both investors here.
That signals this is more than a niche play.
Tenstorrent closed a $1.5B Series E at a $6.2B valuation on October 19.
Flapping Airplanes raised $1B at a $6B valuation on October 21.
No product. No revenue. Just research.

Here's what the pattern tells me. 🔍
Vertical AI — especially legal — is attracting serious institutional capital.
The Legora raise with Nvidia on the cap table says hardware and applications are converging.
And Flapping Airplanes' $1B for early-stage research suggests the current training paradigm has limits.
Investors are covering every angle.

For brand leaders, this funding map is worth studying.
Watch Legora — legal AI at scale will reshape how brands manage contracts.
Cheaper inference from Tenstorrent changes what AI tools cost to run.

Which bet pays off first — vertical AI, hardware infrastructure, or early-stage research?
