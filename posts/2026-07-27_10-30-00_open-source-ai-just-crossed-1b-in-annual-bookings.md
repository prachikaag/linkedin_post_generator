Open-source AI just hit a milestone that changes how brands should think about vendor lock-in. 📊

Together AI closed an $800 million Series C on July 1 at an $8.3 billion valuation. That's more than double where they were 16 months ago. Annual bookings: $1.15 billion last quarter. NVIDIA, Salesforce Ventures, and General Catalyst all joined the round.

But the number isn't the real story.

Together AI exists to make frontier AI run on open-source models. Their pitch is simple: you shouldn't have to go through a single AI provider to get powerful inference. Enterprises pay them to run open models — LLaMA, Mistral, and others — at scale. 💡

The signal I'm watching: the investors in this round aren't just VC money. NVIDIA is in. Salesforce Ventures is in. These are companies with a direct stake in what the AI stack looks like long-term. They're betting open-source infrastructure becomes a standard layer — not a niche choice.

What does this mean for brands? The AI ecosystem is bifurcating. On one side: closed, API-based access from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google. On the other: open infrastructure you can run yourself or through providers like Together. Neither is "better" — they serve different needs and risk profiles.

Brands that haven't had this conversation with their IT and legal teams yet should start now. Your AI vendor strategy is a brand strategy. Who holds your data, your model, your output? That question has a real answer now.

Which side of this equation does your team lean toward — and why?
