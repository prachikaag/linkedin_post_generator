The AI funding boom is real. It's also extremely concentrated. 💰

In H1 2026, North American AI startups shattered investment records. Q1 alone saw $300 billion globally.

Nearly 88% of AI funding went to US companies. Most of that flowed to just two names: OpenAI and Anthropic.

Anthropic closed a $65 billion round in May, at a $965 billion valuation — the most valuable private company globally. OpenAI sits at $852 billion.

Private equity deployed $63 billion into AI, ahead of traditional VC at $38 billion. 📈

Meanwhile: since August 2, 2026, the EU AI Act is in force. Chatbots must disclose they are AI. Deepfakes need labels. AI-generated content must carry machine-readable marks.

Non-compliance fines reach 7% of global annual turnover — higher than GDPR's 4% cap.

Two things are happening in parallel.

Capital is concentrating at the top. A few frontier companies are pulling away from the field. Down the stack, investors now demand proof: defensible data, clear customer need, measurable results.

Vague AI ideas no longer get funded. That's actually a healthy signal.

And the EU AI Act isn't just a compliance checkbox. It's a forcing function for transparency that AI-first brands should have built from day one.

If you use AI in customer-facing products in Europe, the transparency requirements are live now. Audit your AI disclosures today.

For brands evaluating vertical AI tools: follow the funding. Compute, healthcare, legal, and productivity tools with proof are where serious capital is going.
