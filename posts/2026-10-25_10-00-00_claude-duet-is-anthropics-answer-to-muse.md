The race to build always-on AI agents is suddenly very real. 🤖

Anthropic, OpenAI, and Meta all have proactive agents in users' hands now. They don't wait for questions. They monitor, act, and report back.

Anthropic's Claude Duet launched recently as a research preview for Pro and Max subscribers. [1] It sends a morning brief, checks apps every few hours, and flags issues early. It asks for approval before sending messages, making payments, or deleting files.

OpenAI's Dots pursues goals between conversations. Meta's Muse Wonder adds active memory. [2] GPT-6 Sol, now rolling out across ChatGPT, was built for agent tasks. It claims 97% robustness to indirect prompt injection. [3]

The pattern here is unmistakable — AI is moving from reactive tool to proactive teammate. 👀

But most brands are missing something. The approval layer is not a UX footnote. It is the trust architecture. Claude Duet asks before any consequential action. That guardrail matters enormously.

Without it, autonomous agents create liability, not efficiency.

For brand leaders, the question has shifted. It's no longer "should we use AI?" It's "what decisions are you comfortable delegating?"

Map your approval thresholds now. The agents are already in the queue.
