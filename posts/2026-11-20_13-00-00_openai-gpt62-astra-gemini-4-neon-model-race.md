Two flagship models. Twenty-four hours apart. The frontier just levelled up — twice. 🏁

OpenAI launched GPT-6.2 Astra on November 17.
Built for professional work: documents, code, 3D design.
The very next day, Google answered with Gemini 4 Neon.
Three times faster, 60% cheaper, with a 1-million-token context window.

This isn't coincidence. It's the new cadence.

Astra costs under a third of its predecessor per task.
It's the first OpenAI model to reach the High self-improvement threshold.
That milestone also tightened its cybersecurity guardrails (SiliconANGLE, Unite.AI).

Neon scores 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at Extra High thinking.
Claude Opus 5.5 beats it on Terminal-Bench 4.1 and FrontierCode 2.0 (Unite.AI).
Neon reads spreadsheets, PDFs, audio, and video — in a single request.

What interests me more than the benchmarks: the pricing.
20% token cuts from OpenAI. 60% cheaper from Google.
The race to the top is becoming a race to the affordable.

Three frontier models improved in one week — Astra, Neon, Fable 5.2.
The teams integrating AI today will have a year's head start.
The ones still debating model choice in 2027 will be catching up.
