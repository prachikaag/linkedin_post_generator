$1.3 billion for AI inference. $750 million for AI training talent. $240 million for AI in hardware. This isn't hype money. 💰

It's infrastructure money.

Three major rounds closed this past week — and the pattern is different from what I expected. Fal raised $1.3B to expand AI inference infrastructure. Mercor closed a $750M Series D at a $20B valuation, backed by NVIDIA, Salesforce, and Zoom. Nominal raised $240M to bring AI into complex hardware engineering.

The common thread: picks-and-shovels, not chatbot features. 🔧 Fal powers the compute behind AI products. Mercor builds the human-in-the-loop pipelines that make AI training work. Nominal tackles engineering workflows software-only AI can't touch.

One more deal caught my attention: Bay raised $50M for AI agent security after nine months in stealth. The fact that "agent security" is now a fundable category tells you exactly where the industry thinks risk lives.

The "AI is a bubble" argument assumes money is chasing demos. What I'm watching in these deals is money chasing durability — companies solving the operational problems that come after you deploy AI at scale. That's a different bet than it looks from the outside.

For brand and agency teams: the reliability of your AI tools depends on these invisible infrastructure layers. Knowing who powers your vendors' compute isn't nerdy diligence anymore. It's basic risk management.
