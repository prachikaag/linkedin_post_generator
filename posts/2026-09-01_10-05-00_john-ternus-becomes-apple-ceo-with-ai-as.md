Tim Cook built Apple into the world's most valuable company. His successor inherits its biggest challenge. 📱

John Ternus became Apple CEO on September 1. He's a hardware engineer — not a software or AI strategist.

Apple trails every major rival in generative AI. The company is now rebuilding Siri using Google's Gemini as its foundation. That's a significant admission from a company known for doing everything in-house.

Apple's position: strong on hardware, behind on intelligence. Rivals haven't waited.

Anthropic locked in Claude Sonnet 5 at $2 per million input tokens — permanently. Google's Gemini 3.7 Flash launched recently, topping nine coding benchmarks. OpenAI is targeting a public listing this month at a valuation above $1 trillion. On a $25B annual revenue run rate.

McKinsey 2026: organisations scaling AI agents rose from 27% to 40% in one year. Apple is not yet part of that acceleration.

Clay, the AI marketing platform, raised at $7B — up from $5B in eight months. AI for sales and marketing is already mainstream. Apple is catching up.

Rebuilding Siri with Gemini is the pragmatic move. But it signals that Apple's AI ambition now runs through a competitor.

A hardware engineer at the helm may be exactly right. The next AI competition will be fought at the device layer — where Apple still leads.

For brands, watch Apple's September 9 iPhone event closely. How Apple packages AI for mass consumers sets the floor for everyone else.

What Apple does sets the mainstream expectation. And that matters for every brand building on top of it.

Can Apple close the AI gap under Ternus — or has the window already passed?
