Five frontier models dropped in ten days. September just redrew the AI landscape for brands.

Claude Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra, Gemini 3.8 Flash, and DeepSeek V4.1-Flash all launched between September 1 and 10. Not one company. Not one moment. Four providers moving at the same time.

What the evidence shows:

Anthropic cut cached context costs by 75% with Fable 5.1. It added invisible text watermarking and a detection API in private preview. Provenance is becoming a product feature, not an afterthought. ✍️

OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra navigates browsers, spreadsheets and apps as a person would. In a closed press briefing, the company's president closed with: "Welcome to the AGI era." — Greg Brockman, President & Co-founder, OpenAI

Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash scored 90.8% on Terminal-Bench 2.1, up from 81.6% for its predecessor. Priced at $0.75 per million input tokens through December 2026, it's engineered for agentic AI workflows.

DeepSeek's V4.1-Flash — open-weight, MIT licence — reportedly outperforms its own larger flagship V4-Pro at lower cost. 💡 Open-source isn't lagging anymore.

Here's what I think brands are missing: every release this month also lowered prices. The floor dropped. Your AI budget goes further now than it did three weeks ago.

The practical move: audit your vendor contracts before Q4. The pricing terms you agreed to six months ago may no longer reflect the market.
