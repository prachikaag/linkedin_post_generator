44% of U.S. shoppers now avoid brands that use AI in ads.

And the marketing industry just responded by... building more AI.

This week, marketing AI agents went from concept to beta at scale. Sprinklr launched six agents covering ideation, creative, campaigns, compliance, community, and budget. [1] Bluefish extended its agentic platform into paid media. [1] The execution layer is almost fully automated now.

Meanwhile the trust layer is cracking.

The Auros survey confirmed it — consumers are avoiding brands that advertise with AI. [2] Meta now ranks ads using LLMs that read your actual creative. [2] Google is testing personalized ads inside AI Mode search. [3] Lorikeet's AX Index ranked 50 US brands on how well they serve customers' AI agents. [4]

Here's what I see across all of this:

The platforms are accelerating automation. Consumers are pulling back. And most brands are caught in the middle with no transparency strategy.

Even Athena — an AI legal startup backed by a16z — pairs its agents with licensed attorneys. [5] They understand that in trust-sensitive industries, human accountability isn't optional.

Marketing is equally trust-sensitive. Yet AI brand transparency disclosure isn't standard practice.

Brands that get ahead of AI disclosure requirements will build more durable trust. Those that wait will face the backlash instead of leading the conversation.

Should brands be required to disclose when AI wrote or targeted an ad?
