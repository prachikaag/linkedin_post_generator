### Claude (Anthropic)

**Tool:** Claude (claude.ai / Claude for Work)
**Date tested:** Ongoing — first serious use: early 2024
**Category:** Writing, research, strategy, coding

**What I tested:**
Long-form content drafting, research synthesis, building AI workflows,
analysing brand documents, generating campaign frameworks.

**What worked:**
Exceptional at holding context across a long document or conversation.
Follows nuanced instructions better than most — if you write a detailed prompt,
it actually reads the whole thing. Strong at synthesis and structured thinking.

**What didn't:**
Can be verbose by default — needs explicit prompts to be concise.
Occasionally over-hedges on opinions when you want a direct answer.

**Honest verdict:**
The tool I reach for when the task needs careful thinking — strategy documents,
content with real structure, anything where getting the nuance right matters.

**Would you use it again?** Yes

### ChatGPT (OpenAI)

**Tool:** ChatGPT / ChatGPT Plus
**Date tested:** Ongoing since launch
**Category:** Writing, ideation, research, image generation (DALL-E)

**What I tested:**
Content brainstorming, first drafts, social post ideation, image generation for
brand concepts, data analysis with Advanced Data Analysis.

**What worked:**
Fast, conversational, good at quick ideation and first-draft generation.
Broad knowledge base. DALL-E image generation has become genuinely useful for
mood boards and concept visuals.

**What didn't:**
Tends toward safe, generic outputs unless you push hard.
Less consistent when following very detailed brand-specific instructions.

**Honest verdict:**
The Swiss army knife — not always the sharpest tool for any one job,
but reliably useful across the widest range of tasks.

**Would you use it again?** Yes

### Perplexity

**Tool:** Perplexity AI (Pro)
**Date tested:** 2024 onwards
**Category:** Research, news monitoring, fact-checking

**What I tested:**
Real-time news research, competitor analysis, trend spotting, fact-checking
claims before publishing.

**What worked:**
Excellent for cited, real-time research. The fact that it surfaces sources
inline means I can verify claims instantly. Much better than a general search
engine for synthesising information quickly.

**What didn't:**
Not a writing tool — outputs are functional, not branded.
Sometimes misses nuance in how it interprets sources.

**Honest verdict:**
My go-to for research before writing. I use it to build the factual foundation
that I then write on top of.

**Would you use it again?** Yes — daily for research

### Midjourney

**Tool:** Midjourney (v6 / v7)
**Date tested:** Ongoing since v4
**Category:** Image generation, creative direction

**What I tested:**
Brand concept visuals, mood boards, social media images, presentation backgrounds.

**What worked:**
Consistently the highest aesthetic quality of any image generation tool.
Strong at art direction — when you describe a style carefully, it delivers.
Great for concept exploration and presenting visual ideas to clients.

**What didn't:**
Requires significant prompting skill to get consistent results.
Doesn't have a direct integration workflow — the Discord-based interface
adds friction for professional use.

**Honest verdict:**
The best image generation tool for creative quality, but it rewards you for
learning how to prompt it well. There's a learning curve.

**Would you use it again?** Yes — for anything where visual quality matters

### ElevenLabs

**Tool:** ElevenLabs (voice cloning / text-to-speech)
**Date tested:** 2024
**Category:** Audio / voice AI

**What I tested:**
Voice-over generation for video content, creating a custom voice profile,
testing AI voices for explainer videos.

**What worked:**
The voice quality is genuinely remarkable — far better than older TTS tools.
Custom voice cloning worked well with a clean audio sample.
Fast generation, good for iterating on scripts.

**What didn't:**
Emotional range is still limited — it reads well but doesn't always
feel natural in highly conversational, off-the-cuff content.

**Honest verdict:**
Changed how I think about video content. For explainers, course content,
and anything where you need a consistent voice at scale — this is the tool.

**Would you use it again?** Yes — for video and audio content production
