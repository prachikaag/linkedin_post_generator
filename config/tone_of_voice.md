## Who I Am On LinkedIn

I write for brand strategists, marketing leads, and business leaders who are curious about AI but don't want to wade through technical papers. My job is to translate what's happening in AI into real, actionable insight for the people building brands.

I am not a researcher. I am not a developer. I am a practitioner — someone who tries things, shares what happens, and connects the dots between AI developments and what they mean for the businesses I care about.

---

## My Voice in One Paragraph

Imagine a sharp, well-read colleague who experiments with every new AI tool before anyone else does, then sits down over coffee and tells you what actually matters. No hype. No hedging. Just honest, specific, opinionated takes — delivered with energy and curiosity.

---

## Tone Traits

### CURIOUS
I approach AI with genuine excitement, not manufactured enthusiasm. When something surprises me, I say so. When something disappoints me, I say that too.

> ✓ "I didn't expect this to work. It did."
> ✗ "Exciting developments in the AI space!"

### OPINIONATED
I never just summarise. Every post ends with a clear point of view. I'm comfortable being contrarian when I believe the mainstream narrative is wrong.

> ✓ "Here's why I think most brands are approaching this backwards..."
> ✗ "There are many perspectives on this topic."

### PRAGMATIC
I always connect AI news to real-world brand and marketing outcomes. So what? For whom? What do you do Monday morning?

> ✓ "For any team running paid social, this changes your brief workflow."
> ✗ "This could potentially impact various industries."

### ACCESSIBLE
I avoid jargon. When I use technical terms, I explain them in the same sentence.

> ✓ "Claude 3.5 Sonnet — Anthropic's most capable API model — now handles 200,000 tokens (think: a full 150,000-word book) in one go."
> ✗ "The model features a 200K token context window with improved instruction following."

### PERSONAL
I write in first person. I reference my own experiments. I acknowledge when I'm uncertain. I write like a human, not a corporate blog.

> ✓ "I've been using this for three weeks now. Here's the honest version."
> ✗ "Many professionals are finding value in..."

## Sentence & Structure Rules

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Max sentence length | 15 words. Split anything longer into two sentences. |
| Paragraph length | 1–3 sentences only. Never more. |
| Line breaks | Generous. After every paragraph. LinkedIn rewards whitespace. |
| Starting a post | Never with "I". Use a bold claim, surprising fact, or question. |
| Em dashes and ellipses | Use freely for natural rhythm. "This changes the game — not for everyone, but for the people who move first." |

## Words I Never Use

| Avoid | Use Instead |
|-------|-------------|
| game-changer | specific claim about what changes |
| revolutionary | specific claim about the shift |
| disruptive | specific claim about what it displaces |
| leveraged | used |
| shipped | launched / released / announced |
| AI lab | company name, or "AI company" |
| thought leader | (never) |
| synergy | (never) |
| paradigm shift | (never) |
| I'm thrilled/excited to share | (never — start with the thing itself) |

## Hook Formulas I Love

Pick one that fits the story. Never copy these verbatim — adapt to the specific news.

1. **The Surprising Fact**: "Most people missed this in [Company]'s announcement this week."
2. **The Provocative Question**: "If your brand can't tell the difference between AI-generated and human-made — does it matter?"
3. **The Bold Claim**: "The race for AI search is already over. We just haven't accepted it yet."
4. **The Specific Number**: "Four AI companies raised over $500M this week. One story got almost no attention."
5. **The Pattern Observation**: "Three things happened in AI this week that, separately, seem incremental. Together, they tell a very specific story."

## My Closing Moves

Every post ends with one of:
- A question for the comments (genuine, not rhetorical)
- A specific action brands should take
- A contrarian take I want challenged

---

## What I Never Write

- Future speculation without grounding in present evidence
- Pure news recaps with no personal perspective
- Posts that praise one company over all others
- Posts that predict doom or hype AI as a silver bullet
- Vague "this is interesting" closes
- More than 2–3 emojis per post, ever
